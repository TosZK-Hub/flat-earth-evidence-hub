Horizon, Perspective & Ships Hull-Down

One of the community’s most shared “proofs”: a ship or city “should” be behind curvature, yet a long zoom brings hull or skyline back. This page states that claim fairly, then separates perspective, atmospheric refraction, and geometric curvature.

The FE case (steelmanned)

Mark Sargent and many FE experimenters argue: if Earth curves ~8 inches × miles², distant ships, oil platforms and shorelines should sit hundreds of feet “below” the horizon at commonly filmed distances. With modern HD/superzoom cameras (often Nikon P900-class in community culture), operators zoom and report the object returning into frame with little distortion — read as proof the object was never behind a curve, only lost to perspective / resolution.

Related claims: the horizon always rises to eye level no matter how high you go (within ordinary altitudes); therefore there is no geometric drop.

Well-argued hypothesis

Many viral long-zoom clips are real recordings of real distant objects; the debate is interpretation (perspective + refraction vs curvature occlusion), not whether pixels exist.

Globe-model counter-account

On a sphere, an object can be partly geometrically occulted while still sending light that refraction lifts into view (looming / superior mirage), or can be fully above the geometric horizon once observer height and target height are included — so “I can see it after zooming” does not by itself falsify curvature. Perspective shrinks angular size; zoom recovers angular size without proving the surface is a plane.

Classical globe teaching (echoed in Vsauce and Ars Technica explainers): ships tend to disappear hull-first when conditions are ordinary; that pattern is hard to get from pure perspective on a plane without additional ad-hoc rules.

Established evidence

Geometric horizon distance depends on observer height; atmospheric refraction routinely alters apparent horizon; theodolite and GNSS/geodesy networks measure curvature independently of beach photos. A single zoom clip is not a survey.

How to evaluate a viral “zoom-back”

  1. Name the shoot: who filmed, where, date, camera, height above water/ground.
  2. Distance: measured (AIS, map) not guessed.
  3. Heights: observer and target; compute geometric horizon before claiming “impossible.”
  4. Atmosphere: note temperature gradients; mirages can lift or compress scenes.
  5. Label: observation may be established; FE flatness inference remains hypothesis until alternatives are excluded.
Speculation

“They CGI every ship video” or “pilots are sworn to hide the plane horizon” without a named document or whistleblower record.
