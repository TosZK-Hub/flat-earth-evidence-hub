Bedford Level & Water-Surface Experiments

The FE case (steelmanned)

Samuel Birley Rowbotham (writing as “Parallax”) reported in the summer of 1838 that a boat with a flag ~3 ft above water remained visible through a telescope held ~8 inches above the Old Bedford River for the full ~6 miles to Welney Bridge. On a globe with ~25,000-mile circumference, he argued, the mast should have fallen many feet below the line of sight. He published the result in Zetetic Astronomy / Earth Not a Globe and treated still water as a plane.

Modern FE recreations (including Flat Earth UK, 25 June 2016) use long-zoom cameras and lasers over the same canal and report targets or boaters remaining visible farther than “8 inches per mile squared” intuition suggests — taking that as support for a flat water surface.

Well-argued hypothesis

Low-height optical sights over long water stretches can remain consistent with a locally flat appearance; FE authors read that as geometric flatness of Earth.

What survey history adds

In 1870, John Hampden wagered on repeating Rowbotham’s result. Alfred Russel Wallace (trained surveyor) raised the sight line ~13 ft above water to reduce near-surface refraction, added a mid-distance marker, and reported a “bump” consistent with curvature. The referee awarded Wallace the wager; disputes and litigation followed (Hampden was later imprisoned for threats/libel against Wallace).

In 1901, H. Yule Oldham reported three equal-height poles along the Level, with the middle pole appearing higher through a theodolite — taught for decades as a school demonstration of curvature.

In 1904, Lady Elizabeth Blount hired photographer Edgar Clifton; a low camera at Welney reportedly captured a sheet six miles away — republished by FE advocates as flatness evidence.

Established evidence

Atmospheric refraction bends near-horizontal rays; temperature inversions over still water can make a curved surface look flatter or even concave in optical tests. Survey practice accounts for this. Wallace’s and Oldham’s higher sight lines were designed to reduce the worst near-surface effects. See Wikipedia summary citing Garwood (2007) and optics references on the Bedford Level page.

On “8 inches per mile squared”

Community videos often treat 8 in × miles² as a strict disappearance rule. It is a parabolic approximation for small distances, not a complete optical prediction: observer height, target height, and refraction must enter any real line-of-sight calculation. Misusing the slogan alone does not settle either model.

Well-argued hypothesis (contested math)

FE creators sometimes reject the approximation as “globe deception”; globe educators call the bare slogan a misuse. Both sides agree raw “should be invisible” claims need heights and atmosphere stated.

2016 recreation — fair read

The Flat Earth UK video is primary community footage: reeds, laser mis-aim, muggy haze, bridge-height viewing. It is useful as a record of what that group did and claimed. It is not a controlled three-marker theodolite survey. Treat results as hypothesis pending replication with logged heights, levels and weather.

Established

Old Bedford River is a real straight drainage canal; Rowbotham, Wallace, Oldham and Blount ran historically documented tests; refraction strongly affects low optical paths over water; Wallace’s mid-marker method is a classic survey demonstration of curvature under controlled height.

Hypothesis

That low-telescope continuous visibility over six miles proves a globally flat Earth (FE); that every such sight is fully explained by refraction alone without residual flat-model interest (globe-side overclaim if heights aren’t logged).

Open / speculation

Any claim that “NASA lied about canals” or that Wallace “cheated” without primary court/archive specifics beyond the known quarrel with Hampden.
