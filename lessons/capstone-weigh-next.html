Module 6 · Capstone · Lesson 2

Weigh FE vs globe — next steps

In this closing lesson we don’t crown a winner with a meme. We inventory the strongest FE arguments you met, the established measurements that conflict, and what an honest student does next.

Strongest FE arguments in this course (steelmanned)

  • Water / Bedford: low sights over multi-mile canals remaining consistent with a locally flat appearance (Rowbotham; modern Flat Earth UK recreation).
  • Horizon / zoom: long-zoom “bring back” of ships or skylines that naked-eye viewers called gone.
  • Maps: AE / Gleason-style disc presentations used as intuitive FE world maps (community use).
  • Antarctica: Treaty-as-perimeter / restricted-travel narratives in FE media (e.g. Mark Sargent framing).
  • Imagery: suspicion of composites and processing as authenticity problems.
Well-argued hypothesis

Each of the above has named community proponents and deserves a fair statement before criticism.

Established measurements & documents that conflict (no sneer)

  • Survey / optics practice treating near-surface refraction seriously (Wallace-style raised sights; Bedford history).
  • Spherical geometry for distance/drop under stated radius assumptions (lab calculator).
  • AE as a standard map projection of a sphere (not proof of a disc).
  • Antarctic Treaty primary text and documented tourism/research logistics (ats.aq and operators).
  • Documented Blue Marble / MODIS visualization pipelines (e.g. Robert Simmon interviews).
Established evidence

These are multi-method or primary-document anchors. They do not license contempt for people who still find FE arguments interesting.

What remains open / speculation

Speculation

Unnamed controllers, dome builders, and motive-only stories without documents. Keep them labelled or cut.

Your next honest steps

  1. Re-run the claim tester on a new video from the classroom shelf.
  2. Pick one theme in the research library and add a local reflection note.
  3. When you share a claim, include heights, distance, date, and a primary link — or admit you don’t have them.
Course closing line. Curiosity over contempt. Steelman without “proven.” Skeptical of every narrative — including this classroom’s.
