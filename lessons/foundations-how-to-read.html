Module 1 · Foundations · Lesson 1

How to read claims

In this lesson we practice the only skill that makes the rest of the course honest: separating what is measured from what is argued from what is guessed.

The three labels (memorise these)

Established evidence

Multi-method consensus measurements, primary treaties, documented image pipelines, standard projection definitions — including globe-model results that conflict with an FE claim. Strong evidence, not “closed forever.”

Well-argued hypothesis

FE models and contested readings (low canal sights as flatness; AE disc as physical Earth map; spotlight sun) with named proponents and real critics.

Speculation / metaphor

Hidden dome builders, motive-only cabals, “turned away at gunpoint” stories without named primary records. Marked — never sold as fact.

Steelman first

Before answering an FE claim, state the community’s best case in its own terms. Before accepting a mainstream dismissal, check whether it skipped heights, atmosphere, or the primary document. Slogans on every side fail this classroom.

Well-argued hypothesis (method)

Steelmanning is a reading discipline, not an endorsement that flat Earth is proven science.

What a fair claim always states

  • Observer height, target height, distance (for optical / horizon claims).
  • Named experimenter, date, place, and what was logged.
  • Whether the media is a raw capture, a composite, or a visualisation.
  • What result would change the author’s mind.
Globe conflict reminder. When multi-method geodesy, survey practice allowing for refraction, or primary treaty text conflicts with an FE reading, we put that under established evidence without sneering — and still steelman the FE argument on its own page.
